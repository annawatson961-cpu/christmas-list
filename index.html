Anna Marie's Super Wow Grand Gift

The gift I want most can't be wrapped.

Dad and Kim, this one is just for you two. It's not on a shelf anywhere. It's a few days away with my brothers and sisters, just us kids, and it can only happen with your help.

There's a little story first. Keep scrolling.

Remember us?

Look at us. All of us, blended together as one. Somewhere along the way, kids from two families became brothers and sisters. That didn't happen by accident. You guys made it happen, one holiday, one road trip, one Christmas morning at a time.

Now we're all grown. We have jobs, some of us have spouses, some have kids of their own, and calendars that never seem to align. We still love each other just as much. We just don't get to be together the way we were in these pictures… and I miss it.

Then vs. now

I did the math. Well, my best guess at it. Here's what has happened to our time together.

17 years

How long it would take us today to get the time together we used to have in a single year.

~680 days

Together before Ashley left for college. That's almost two whole years.

45 minutes

The farthest any of us lives from the rest. Distance isn't what keeps us apart. Busy lives do.

4 out of 5

Of all the days we'll ever spend together, that's how many are already behind us if nothing changes.

One 4-night trip would give us as much time together as a whole year does right now.

How I counted

These are my best estimates from what I remember, not exact records.

YearsWhat changedDays a year

Back then: every other weekend during the school year (about 40 days), every other week all summer (about 35), holidays (about 6) and a family trip every other year (about 3 a year on average).

After that: I counted only the days all five of us were actually together, like holidays, school breaks, summers and trips.

4 out of 5: about 990 days together so far, compared with about 250 more if we keep seeing each other around 5 days a year for the next 50 years.

Why now, not someday

I don't want to wait two or three more years for this. I want us to start planning this Christmas and go in 2027.

Look at that chart. Every year we put it off, there are more schedules to work around and fewer days to work with. By next year there will be seven grandkids. Life isn't going to slow down on its own, so the best time to start is right now.

My Christmas wish

A siblings-only trip. All of us kids, together, for a long weekend or a few days. No spouses, no to-do lists, no rushing. Just time to laugh like we did in these pictures and get to really know each other as the adults we've become.

Here's the honest part: I can't make this happen on my own. When an invitation comes from me, it gets lost in the group chat. When it comes from Dad and Kim, everyone finds a way. That's why I'm asking you.

To be clear, I'm not asking you to pay for this.We'll each cover our own way. What I need is your support: your buy-in, your voice to get everyone on board, and your help actually making it happen.

What I'm asking for

  1. Get it started. Send the invitation and tell everyone it matters to you. Your word is what gets all of us to say yes. I'm happy to write the message for you.
  2. Help us lock in the dates. Help us pick a weekend that works, then hold us to it.
  3. Host a grandparents' weekend. Watch the grandkids for those few nights so their parents can come too.

Why this matters so much to me

Time is the one thing we can't get back.

We'll never be exactly this age, all together, again. Right now the kids are little, everyone is healthy, and we can still say yes. Every year we wait, it gets a little harder to pull off.

We're a blended family, so this matters even more.

We didn't all grow up under one roof the whole time. The bond between us was built on purpose, by you. This trip is how we keep building it ourselves, as adults, so it lasts long after we're the ones hosting Christmas.

Experiences outlast things.

Psychologists Leaf Van Boven and Tom Gilovich found that experiences bring people more lasting happiness than possessions do. We keep reliving and retelling them, and they become part of who we are. Everything else on my list will wear out someday. This won't.

Close relationships are what make a good life.

The Harvard Study of Adult Development has followed people's lives for more than 80 years, and its clearest finding is that close relationships keep us happier and healthier as we age. My brothers and sisters are the people I'll know the longest of anyone in my life. I want us to stay close for all of it.

It's your legacy.

You spent years bringing two families together into one. This is what that looks like when it works: your kids choosing each other, on purpose, as grown-ups.
